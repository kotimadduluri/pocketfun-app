Privacy policy

Pocket Fun, by Koteswara Rao. Effective 30 October 2026.

This policy explains what the Pocket Fun app for Android and iOS does with your data. In short: your games and settings stay on your phone, there are no accounts, and we have no servers. Google receives some data to show ads and, unless you switch it off, to tell us about crashes and how the games are played.

The short version

  • Your saves, scores, names and settings stay on your phone. We cannot see them.
  • No accounts, no purchases and no location permission.
  • Google shows the ads and, unless you switch it off, receives crash reports and play stats.
  • You can switch off crash reports and play stats, and say no to personalised ads.
  • The app is for people aged 13 and over.

Who we are

Pocket Fun is published by Koteswara Rao, based in the United Kingdom. We decide why and how the data in this policy is used. Data protection law calls that the "controller". Contact: maddulurikoti@gmail.com.

What stays on your device

To work, the app stores the following on your phone or tablet:

  • Game progress, so you can continue a game later.
  • Best scores.
  • Your settings.
  • Player names you type in for Ludo.
  • Your stats: games played, time played and results. The app keeps a short on-device log of activity to build them.
  • Your choices about ads and about sharing crash reports and play stats.

We cannot see or access this data. It is not sent to us. We do not ask for your name, email or location, and the app does not ask for location permission.

What is sent, to whom, and why

The app uses three services from Google. We do not run any servers of our own.

Ads: Google AdMob

The app shows banner ads from Google AdMob on the Home and Games screens. Ads are never shown while you play a game, or in Settings. To show ads and measure how they do, Google may collect:

  • your advertising ID, a resettable number on your phone that advertisers use instead of your name;
  • your IP address, which Google can use to work out roughly where you are (for example your city), not your exact position;
  • details about your device and the app, and how you interact with ads.

Google acts on its own account for this ad data. See policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites and policies.google.com/privacy.

Crash reports: Firebase Crashlytics

When the app has a problem or crashes, a report can be sent to Google's Firebase Crashlytics. It contains the type of error, where in the app's code it happened, and your phone model, system version and app version. We keep error messages and anything you typed out of these reports. Crashlytics also uses a random installation ID that Google creates for this app on your device.

Play stats: Firebase Analytics

The app can send play stats to Google's Firebase Analytics. They tell us which screens and games are used and how: the app opening, screens viewed, a game starting, ending, being paused or quit, its result, how long it lasted, its difficulty, new bests, and settings you change. They never include your name, player names, saves or anything you typed. Firebase uses a random app ID created for this app on your device. Because Google receives your IP address with each upload, it can work out your approximate location. We do not set up user accounts or user profiles in Firebase, and Firebase is set up not to read your advertising ID.

We can see the crash reports and play stats in Google's Firebase console. We use them only to fix problems and improve the games.

Your choice: crash reports and play stats

In the app, go to Settings, then Privacy & storage, and use Share crash reports and play stats. In the EEA, UK and Switzerland it starts off, and the app asks you once whether to turn it on. Everywhere else it starts on. When you turn it off, nothing is sent to Firebase, unsent crash reports are deleted, and the app gets a new random ID. Your choice is kept when you use Delete all data. If you restore the app from an Android backup, the setting goes back to the default for your region, so check it again.

Personalised ads and how to say no

Personalised ads use your advertising ID and your activity across apps to match your interests. Otherwise ads are less relevant, but you see the same number of them.

  • Google's consent form. Where the law requires it, the app asks for your consent before it requests any ads. You can review or change your answer under Settings > Privacy & storage > Ad privacy. This row only appears where the form is required.
  • On iOS, after Google's form, Apple's prompt asks whether Pocket Fun may track you across other companies' apps and websites. You can change this later: Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking, then turn off Pocket Fun (or turn off Allow Apps to Request to Track for every app). Ads still appear if you say no.
  • On Android: Settings > Privacy > Ads > Delete advertising ID (on some phones: Settings > Google > Ads).

How long data is kept

  • On your device: until you delete it or uninstall the app. We hold no copies.
  • Crash reports are kept by Google for 90 days, then deleted from its live and backup systems (firebase.google.com/support/privacy).
  • Play stats are kept for 14 months, then deleted.
  • Ad data is kept under Google's own policies (policies.google.com/privacy).

Turning off sharing deletes unsent crash reports and resets the Firebase app ID, which cuts the link to earlier data from this device.

Moving data to other countries

Google processes data in the United States and other countries, which may have different data protection rules from yours. Google takes part in the EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Data Privacy Frameworks and the UK Extension (firebase.google.com/support/privacy).

Device backups

If you have your device's own backup turned on (Google's Android backup, or iCloud backup on iOS), that backup may include this app's data. It is stored under your own Google or Apple account under their terms, and we cannot access it. Deleting data in the app does not remove copies already in a backup.

Your rights, wherever you live

Depending on your country (for example the EEA, UK, Switzerland, US states, Brazil, Canada, India, Japan, South Korea or Australia), you may have the right to access, correct or delete your data, object to or limit its use, take a copy, withdraw consent, and complain to your data protection authority. We are based in the UK, where that is the Information Commissioner’s Office (ico.org.uk). To use these rights, contact maddulurikoti@gmail.com. Because we hold no account or profile, we usually cannot identify you from what Google holds, so the controls in this policy are often the fastest way.

US states. We do not sell your data for money. Sharing your advertising ID with Google for personalised ads may count as "sharing" for targeted advertising under some state laws. To opt out, use the device settings described under personalised ads above.

For people in the EEA, UK and Switzerland

The law requires us to have a reason, called a "legal basis", for using your data.

  • Ads: your consent, given through Google's consent form. Consent means you said yes freely and can say no again at any time under Ad privacy.
  • Crash reports and play stats: your consent. The switch starts off, nothing is sent until you turn it on, and you can turn it off again at any time.
  • Everything kept on your device: needed to run the app you asked for.

Deleting your data

  • Use Delete all data in Settings, Privacy & storage, to remove progress, bests, stats and settings from your device. It also resets the Firebase app ID and removes unsent crash reports.
  • Uninstalling the app removes its data from your device.
  • You can reset or delete your advertising ID in your device settings.
  • To ask us about data Google holds from this app, contact us.

Children

Pocket Fun is for people aged 13 and over. It is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect their data. We do not check your age. If you are under 16 and live where the age for agreeing to this is higher than 13, ask a parent before you agree to anything in the app.

What the app does not have

  • No accounts or sign-in.
  • No in-app purchases.
  • No servers run by us.
  • No location permission.

Security

Data sent from the app to Google travels over encrypted connections. Your saves stay in the app's private storage on your device, protected by your phone's own security. No method is perfectly secure, so we cannot promise absolute security.
