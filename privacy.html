Privacy policy

Pocket Fun, by Koteswara Rao. Effective 31 October 2026.

This policy explains what the Pocket Fun app for Android and iOS does with your data. In short: your games and settings stay on your phone, there are no accounts, and we have no servers. Google receives some data to show ads and, only if you say yes, to tell us about crashes and how the games are played.

The short version

  • Your saves, player names and settings stay on your phone. We cannot see them.
  • No accounts, no purchases and no location permission.
  • Google shows the ads. Crash reports and play stats go to Google only if you turn sharing on.
  • You can turn sharing off again at any time, and say no to personalised ads.
  • The app is for people aged 18 and over.

Who we are

Pocket Fun is published by Koteswara Rao, based in the United Kingdom. We decide why and how the data in this policy is used. Data protection law calls that the "controller". Contact: maddulurikoti@gmail.com.

What stays on your device

To work, the app stores the following on your phone or tablet:

  • Game progress, so you can continue a game later.
  • Best scores.
  • Your settings.
  • Player names you type in for Ludo.
  • Your stats: games played, time played and results. To build them, the app keeps a log of your recent in-app actions with their times, and which days you played.
  • Your choices about ads and about sharing crash reports and play stats.

We cannot see or access this data, and it is not sent to us. If sharing is on, some play stats (such as a game's result or a new best) also go to Google, as described below. We do not ask for your name, email or location, and the app does not ask for location permission.

What is sent, to whom, and why

The app uses services from Google: AdMob with its consent tool, Firebase Crashlytics and Firebase Analytics. We do not run any servers of our own. Apart from Google, as described below, no one receives data from the app.

Ads: Google AdMob

The app shows banner ads from Google AdMob on the Home and Games screens. It does not show them while you play a game or in Settings. Each time the app opens, Google's consent tool checks with Google whether a consent form applies where you are. To show ads and measure how they do, Google may collect:

  • your advertising ID, a resettable number on your phone that advertisers use instead of your name (on iOS, only if you allow tracking);
  • your IP address, which Google can use to work out roughly where you are (for example your city), not your exact position;
  • other identifiers Google creates for apps on your device (such as Android's app set ID), details about your device, network and the app, how you interact with ads, and diagnostics about the ad software.

For ad data, Google says it acts as its own controller, under its own policies. See policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites and policies.google.com/privacy. Where Google's consent form appears, it lists the ad companies that may receive data. You can see that list in the form.

Crash reports: Firebase Crashlytics

If sharing is on and the app has a problem or crashes, a report is sent to Google's Firebase Crashlytics. It contains the type of error, where in the app's code it happened, your phone model, system version and app version, the phone's state at the time (such as free memory, storage, battery and orientation, and whether it is rooted or jailbroken), and the app's last few actions before the problem. The app removes error messages from the reports it creates, and it does not add anything you typed. Crashlytics uses random installation IDs that Google creates for this app on your device.

Play stats: Firebase Analytics

If sharing is on, the app sends play stats to Google's Firebase Analytics. They tell us which screens and games are used and how: the app opening, screens viewed, a game starting, ending, being paused, resumed or quit, its result, how long it lasted, its difficulty, new bests, and settings you change. Firebase also records some things on its own, such as the first time the app opens, how long it is used, app and system updates, and your phone model, system version, language and app version. The app is set up so play stats do not include your name, player names, saves or anything you typed. Firebase uses a random app ID created for this app on your device. Because Google receives your IP address with each upload, it can work out your approximate location. We do not set up user accounts or user profiles in Firebase, and we set Firebase not to use your advertising ID.

We can see the crash reports and play stats in Google's Firebase console. We use them to fix problems and improve the games.

Your choice: crash reports and play stats

Sharing starts off. The app asks you on the Home screen whether to share crash reports and play stats, until you choose Share or No thanks. The app does not send crash reports or play stats before you say yes. You can change your answer at any time in Settings, then Privacy & storage, with Share crash reports and play stats. When you turn it off, the app stops sending data to Firebase, deletes crash reports not yet sent, and resets the Analytics app ID. Your choice is kept when you use Delete all data. If you restore the app from an Android backup, sharing goes back to off.

Personalised ads and how to say no

Personalised ads use your advertising ID and your activity across apps to match your interests. If you say no, ads are less relevant, but Google still receives some data to show them, count them and stop fraud: your IP address, device and app details, and how you interact with ads.

  • Google's consent form. Where the law requires it (for example in the EEA, the UK, Switzerland and some US states), the app shows Google's form before it asks Google for ads. You can review or change your answer under Settings > Privacy & storage > Ad privacy. This row appears only where the form applies.
  • On iOS, after Google's form, Apple's prompt asks whether Pocket Fun may track you across other companies' apps and websites. You can change this later in your iPhone's Settings, under Privacy & Security, then Tracking. Ads still appear if you say no.
  • On Android, you can reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings, usually under Privacy, then Ads, or under Google, then Ads. The exact menu names vary by phone and system version.

How long data is kept

  • On your device: until you delete it or uninstall the app. We do not keep copies on any server of ours.
  • Crash reports: Google keeps them for 90 days, then starts removing them from its live and backup systems (firebase.google.com/support/privacy).
  • Play stats: our Analytics settings keep detailed data linked to the app ID for up to 14 months, after which Google deletes it. Summary reports with no IDs may be kept longer.
  • Ad data: under Google's own policies (policies.google.com/privacy).

Turning off sharing resets the Analytics app ID, so new play stats from this device are no longer linked to earlier ones.

Moving data to other countries

Google processes data in the United States and other countries, which may have different data protection rules from yours. Google says it complies with the EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Data Privacy Frameworks and the UK Extension (firebase.google.com/support/privacy).

Backups

If you have your device's own backup turned on (Google's Android backup, or iCloud backup on iOS), that backup may include this app's data. It is stored under your own Google or Apple account under their terms, and we cannot access it. Deleting data in the app does not remove copies already in a backup. If you use Export backup in Settings, the file goes where you choose, and it is your copy to manage.

Your rights, wherever you live

Depending on your country (for example the EEA, UK, Switzerland, US states, Brazil, Canada, India, Japan, South Korea or Australia), you may have the right to access, correct or delete your data, object to or limit its use, take a copy, withdraw consent, and complain to a data protection authority, either where you live or in the UK, where we are based: the Information Commissioner’s Office (ico.org.uk). To use these rights, contact maddulurikoti@gmail.com. We aim to reply within one month. Because we hold no account or profile, we usually cannot identify you from what Google holds, so the controls in this policy are often the fastest way, but we will help where we can.

You do not have to give us any data to use the app. We do not make decisions about you that have legal or similarly significant effects based only on automated processing.

US states. We do not sell your personal information for money. Using your advertising ID and device data for personalised ads may count as a "sale", "sharing" or "targeted advertising" under some state laws. Where those laws apply, Google's consent form gives you the choice to opt out, and you can change it later under Ad privacy. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID as described above.

For people in the EEA, UK and Switzerland

The law requires us to have a reason, called a "legal basis", for using your data.

  • Ads: your consent, given through Google's consent form. You can say no again at any time under Ad privacy. Some ad companies say they may also rely on their own "legitimate interest". In Google's form that is off unless you turn it on under Manage options, and you can object to it there.
  • Crash reports and play stats: your consent. Sharing starts off, and you can turn it off again at any time.
  • Everything kept on your device: needed to run the app you asked for.

Deleting your data

  • Use Delete all data in Settings, Privacy & storage, to remove progress, bests, stats and settings from your device. It also resets the Analytics app ID and deletes crash reports not yet sent.
  • Uninstalling the app removes its data from your device.
  • You can reset or delete your advertising ID in your device settings.

Delete all data does not change your choices about ads or sharing. You can change those in Settings, Privacy & storage, at any time.

We cannot find or delete data Google already holds from this app, because we hold no account or name to look it up by. After Delete all data, new data from your device is no longer linked to it, and Google deletes it on the schedule in "How long data is kept". For ad data, use Google's controls (policies.google.com/privacy). Questions: maddulurikoti@gmail.com.

Children

Pocket Fun is for people aged 18 and over. It is not directed to children, and we do not knowingly collect data from anyone under 18. We do not check your age. If you believe someone under 18 has used the app and data was sent to Google, contact us. Use Delete all data in Settings on that device; we will explain what else can be done.

What this version of the app does not have

  • No accounts or sign-in.
  • No in-app purchases.
  • No servers run by us.
  • No location permission.

Security

The Google services the app uses send data over encrypted connections. Your saves stay in the app's private storage on your device, protected by your phone's own security. No method is perfectly secure, so we cannot promise absolute security.
